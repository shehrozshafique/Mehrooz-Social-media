# Caption: $9,043 in 4 months with GoHighLevel

I never thought I'd earn this much from a tool I didn't even know existed when I started.

$9,043 in the last 4 months with GoHighLevel.

But the most valuable lessons weren't about GHL itself.
Here's what I wish someone had told me earlier 👇

1️⃣ Take the job you're still learning.
Not one you know nothing about. Don't fake being an expert.
But if you understand the basics and believe you can figure it out, take the challenge.
Research. Learn. Get frustrated. Solve it.
Deliver the best work you can, and never promise what you can't keep.
That's how real skills are built. Not by watching 50 tutorials and waiting to "feel ready."
It's also how you build your first portfolio.

2️⃣ Your first client is worth more than what they pay.
You're not only getting paid. You're getting:
→ Experience
→ A portfolio piece
→ A testimonial
→ Someone who may trust you with the next project
So I always gave my best, even on small budgets.
One happy client can introduce you to the one who changes everything.

3️⃣ Be the person who figures things out.
Clients don't care how many tutorials you've watched or tools you know.
They have a problem, and they want it solved.
Sometimes you'll spend hours on something that looks "simple" to them. That's fine.
They're not paying you to know everything.
They're paying you to own the problem.
(Just be honest about what you can and can't do.)

4️⃣ Don't underestimate relationships.
Getting a client is one thing. Getting them to come back is another.
Communicate clearly. Meet deadlines.
Don't disappear when it gets hard.
If something goes wrong, tell them.
These "boring" things matter more than people think.

5️⃣ Let your work speak louder than your proposals.
In the beginning, I had to convince people to give me a chance.
Now, a lot of my work comes from referrals.
That's the best feeling as a freelancer: someone trusts you because another client had a great experience.
Not because of a fancy sales pitch.

I'm still learning, and there's a lot I don't know yet.
But these lessons helped me most along the way.
